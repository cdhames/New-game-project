# Concept Selection

## Decision status

**Selected:** The Long Map (working title). The name is provisional; the core shared-knowledge premise is a design lock pending prototype validation.

## Criteria

Concepts were judged on internet-native necessity, meaningful asynchronous interaction, solo and cold-start viability, short-session clarity, systemic depth, visual identity, ethical engagement, simulation value, feasible web delivery, accessibility potential, and ability to prove or disprove the premise in a small vertical slice.

## Finalists

- **The Long Map:** short expeditions produce limited Observations; players selectively publish Reports into an aging, corroborated public Atlas over hidden Ground truth.
- **Archive Shift:** players collectively classify and restore a changing digital archive, with cooperation expressed through prior curatorial choices.
- **Floodline:** players prepare interconnected districts for recurring environmental change, inheriting consequences from asynchronous plans.
- **Signal Choir:** players contribute constrained signal fragments to a shared interpretive system that changes what later players can perceive.
- **Borrowed City:** players temporarily steward spaces whose rules and histories accumulate across many short tenures.

These summaries describe selection-stage concepts only; rejected concepts are not a source of mechanics or terminology for this project.

## Why The Long Map

The Atlas makes the internet connection foundational: shared knowledge is the play surface, not an attached leaderboard or chat layer. Selective disclosure creates cooperation without requiring synchronous population, unrestricted text, or direct conflict. Hidden Ground truth, visible provenance, Report age, corroboration, and Drift create a testable trust problem. The cartographic interface supplies a distinctive visual object, while short Expeditions keep the first prototype bounded. The same deterministic rules can support bots, balance studies, replay, and a credible cold start.

## Why alternatives were rejected

- **Archive Shift** risked turning into content-heavy classification work with a weaker moment-to-moment journey.
- **Floodline** implied a broader civic simulation and longer feedback loops than a small first playable could validate.
- **Signal Choir** offered strong atmosphere but less immediately legible decisions and accessibility risks around abstract interpretation.
- **Borrowed City** required substantially more authored spatial content and governance rules before its distinctive promise could be tested.

The alternatives are rejected, not queued features.

## Prototype assumptions

The MVP must test whether incomplete but legible information produces better route decisions rather than confusion; whether selective publication creates genuine tradeoffs; whether stale Reports feel fair; whether a three-to-seven-minute Expedition supports tension and return decisions; whether the Atlas visibly remembers a prior session; whether bots can sustain utility without deceptive social proof; and whether the map remains readable on desktop, mobile, keyboard, and assistive technology. Failure gates are defined in the [MVP specification](MVP_SPEC.md).
