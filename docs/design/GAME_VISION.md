# Game Vision

## Player promise

**One-sentence pitch:** Explore a shifting archipelago in short Expeditions, decide what to reveal, and navigate an Atlas built from knowledge no single player can possess.

The player fantasy is to be a field explorer and careful cartographer: preparing for uncertainty, noticing what others missed, returning with scarce evidence, and leaving useful knowledge that changes later journeys.

## Emotional arc

A session should move from curiosity and informed uncertainty, through mounting commitment and discovery, into a tense press-on-or-return choice, then relief, reflection, and social consequence as Observations become Reports. Long-term emotion should alternate between familiarity and renewed mystery as Drift changes trusted regions without erasing their histories.

## Distinctive identity and internet-native foundation

The Atlas is simultaneously interface, shared artifact, and multiplayer system. The server holds hidden Ground truth; players see personal Observations and structured Reports with age, provenance, confidence, and corroboration. No omniscient global map exists. Prior players matter even when absent, while a lone human can complete the full loop.

This imperfect, persistent knowledge commons is the central **design lock**. The working title, exact setting details, values, and presentation techniques are provisional.

## Target first session

A guest reaches a meaningful route or loadout decision in roughly 30 seconds, starts from a Waystation, completes a three-to-seven-minute Expedition, chooses whether to return, publishes at least one Report, and sees the Atlas change. The player should understand that Reports are useful claims rather than guaranteed truth and should have a reason to begin a second Expedition.

## Design pillars

1. **Knowledge has cost.** Observation consumes supplies, time, safety, or opportunity.
2. **Trust is playable.** Age, provenance, confidence, corroboration, and Drift affect decisions.
3. **Every return leaves a trace of value.** Personal success and public contribution overlap but are not identical.
4. **Asynchronous by nature.** Shared consequences enrich solo-time play without scheduled attendance.
5. **Short journeys, persistent world.** Each Expedition resolves quickly; the Atlas, Waystations, Traces, and Logbook create continuity.
6. **Legible uncertainty.** Risk is communicated honestly enough for informed choices, including accessible non-color cues.

## Anti-pillars

The game is not an omniscient-map optimization puzzle, synchronous competitive arena, unrestricted social network, deception simulator, vertical-power treadmill, content treadmill disguised by timers, real-money economy, or mandatory-account funnel. It must not reward fabricated Reports, manipulative retention, harassment, or overwhelming new players with veteran power.

## Ethical engagement

Engagement should come from curiosity, mastery, stewardship, and an evolving shared artifact. No monetization, paid advantage, manipulative energy timers, fake scarcity, deceptive synthetic social proof, or mandatory registration belongs in the MVP. Session stopping points must be respected. Structured symbols replace public free text at launch. Telemetry follows the privacy limits in [Simulation and Telemetry](../testing/SIMULATION_AND_TELEMETRY.md).

## Visual direction

A restrained original cartographic aesthetic combines a dark shifting sea, luminous ink, topographic lines, layered annotations, map glyphs, subtle currents and weather, and illustrated encounter panels. The map should be the identifying visual object. Strong hierarchy, scalable text, reduced-motion behavior, non-color status encoding, keyboard access, and readable desktop/mobile composition are requirements. Only original, commissioned, or appropriately licensed art may be used.

## Long-term possibility space

After validation, the world may support richer instruments, regional ecologies, longer histories, collective Waystation specializations, structured expedition goals, Trace recovery networks, and constrained social coordination. These are possibilities, not launch commitments; each must preserve knowledge uncertainty and horizontal progression.

## Major risks

- Uncertainty may read as noise or unfairness rather than strategy.
- Publishing may have an obvious optimum or feel altruistic without personal relevance.
- Drift may invalidate effort too quickly or allow the Atlas to become static if too slow.
- Low population may make corroboration sparse; synthetic activity may feel misleading if poorly framed.
- Repetition may surface before the knowledge layer creates durable variety.
- The map may become visually dense or inaccessible on small screens and assistive technology.
- Server authority and persistent histories may create operational, moderation, privacy, and cost burdens.

The [MVP](MVP_SPEC.md) exists to test these risks before content or infrastructure expansion.
