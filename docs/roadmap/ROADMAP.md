# Roadmap

This evidence-gated roadmap makes no calendar promises. Each phase can pause, narrow, or revise direction before further investment.

## Phase 0: Authoritative design baseline

**Goals:** establish mandate, vision, central systems, MVP proof obligations, technical boundaries, simulation strategy, and decision records.

**Outputs:** the documentation indexed in [docs/README.md](../README.md).

**Validation gate:** terminology and decisions are coherent; scope is implementable; no hidden dependency on population, infrastructure, or final branding.

**Pause/revise if:** the premise cannot be expressed as a bounded test, conflicts remain unresolved, or owner-only legal/financial authority is required.

## Phase 1: Local deterministic core-loop prototype

**Goals:** prove a single local Expedition, incomplete route planning, Observation cost, return/failure, selective publication, and replay.

**Outputs:** pure game core, versioned protocol, minimal accessible browser interface, seeded scenarios, tests, and replay fixtures.

**Validation gate:** deterministic acceptance targets and basic usability loop pass without network or database.

**Current evidence:** the deterministic core and Revision 0.2 browser prototype are technically
complete on the unmerged feature branch. Revision 0.2 improved interface structure, route evidence,
resource explanations, and outcome clarity. The
[second owner playtest](../playtests/PLAYTEST-0002-owner.md) nevertheless failed the engagement and
core-fantasy gate: the loop felt like data retrieval and administrative filing, with weak world
feedback and no compelling emotional reason for another Expedition.

Phase 1 remains open. One bounded, unimplemented
[Revision 0.3 adventure-first experiment](../design/REVISION_0.3_ADVENTURE_FIRST_PIVOT.md) is
authorized around The Bell Beneath North Mark. It must test a concrete encounter, named discovery,
capability reward, visible Atlas and Drift change, strategic reveal choice, and one simulated outside
claim before any broader content, social, or infrastructure expansion.

**Pause/revise if:** uncertainty is unintelligible, choices collapse, replay cannot remain
deterministic, scope exceeds the bounded slice, or the adventure-first test still feels like a
workflow and fails to create credible desire for another Expedition.

## Phase 2: Simulated shared Atlas and bot testing

**Goals:** test aging, corroboration, Drift, Traces, Waystation contribution, strategy diversity, exploits, and cold start.

**Outputs:** six core bot policies, Monte Carlo runner, scenario suites, metrics reports, and tuning proposals.

**Validation gate:** multiple viable strategies, bounded resources, useful low-population Atlas, visible staleness, reproducible runs.

**Pause/revise if:** one strategy dominates robustly, synthetic activity must deceive to work, or information becomes noise/omniscience.

## Phase 3: Local multi-client/server-authoritative prototype

**Goals:** validate authority, hidden-state protection, command concurrency, idempotency, and asynchronous cross-client consequences.

**Outputs:** local API, replaceable persistence adapter, two-client tests, threat model, contract and workflow tests.

**Validation gate:** clients cannot reveal or mutate unauthorized Ground truth; later sessions reliably reflect valid events.

**Pause/revise if:** authority costs overwhelm the loop, consistency defects undermine trust, or security design requires premature infrastructure.

## Phase 4: Persistent development environment

**Goals:** validate durable identity, storage evolution, migrations, observability, recovery, and bounded operating cost.

**Outputs:** owner-approved development infrastructure, persistent test world, backup/restore exercise, cost and shutdown controls, privacy review.

**Validation gate:** safe recovery and migrations, acceptable measured cost, protected secrets, reliable persistent histories.

**Pause/revise if:** owner approval is absent, recurring cost is unjustified, privacy/security risks lack mitigation, or operations outpace product evidence.

## Phase 5: Closed human playtest

**Goals:** test comprehension, emotion, trust, publication choices, cold-start honesty, session length, and return motivation with diverse players.

**Outputs:** consented test plan, accessibility-inclusive sessions, minimized telemetry, qualitative findings, prioritized revisions.

**Validation gate:** players use Atlas evidence, understand staleness, experience meaningful tradeoffs, and can complete accessible workflows.

**Pause/revise if:** central terminology or trust remains confusing, contribution lacks value, Drift feels unfair, or harmful engagement/social patterns appear.

## Phase 6: Visual, balance, accessibility, and onboarding refinement

**Goals:** make the map identifying and readable, tighten first-session flow, broaden viable strategies, and meet defined accessibility criteria.

**Outputs:** original visual system, responsive/reduced-motion modes, instrument and scenario tuning, onboarding improvements, regression suites.

**Validation gate:** usability and accessibility targets pass across supported devices; visual density and balance remain legible.

**Pause/revise if:** polish conceals unresolved core problems, accessibility requires a fundamental interaction redesign, or content burden is unsustainable.

## Phase 7: Launch-readiness work

**Goals:** establish lawful branding, security, privacy, moderation, reliability, support, capacity, incident response, and owner-approved deployment.

**Outputs:** cleared name/assets, production threat review, policies, runbooks, load/recovery tests, cost plan, launch/rollback criteria.

**Validation gate:** owner approvals are complete; security, accessibility, reliability, privacy, operational, and product evidence meet explicit thresholds.

**Pause/revise if:** legal clearance, consent, funding, safety, reliability, or sustainable operations are unresolved. No merge or launch is implied by reaching this phase.
